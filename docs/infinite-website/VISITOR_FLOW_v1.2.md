# GENEZISI — THE INFINITE WEBSITE
## Complete B → C Visitor Journey / Creative Storyboard v1.2

**Date:** 29 October 2026  
**Status:** Founder-endorsed complete narrative **direction and sequence**; exact scene copy, animation treatment, integration, production readiness and gate sign-offs are still subject to staged review. Not a claim that the full story is implemented.  
**Basis:** The consolidated [North Star v1.2](./NORTH_STAR_v1.2.md) and founder decisions on 29 October: **centered opening → interface pushes the opening left → fully settled B → forward spatial journey → centered C reveal → genuine Selected Work**. This supersedes the old static B opening while retaining the controlling North Star's essential business, accessibility, truthfulness and handoff constraints. If details conflict, North Star v1.2 controls.

## One sentence

**We begin with clarity, travel through the system behind a useful digital experience, and emerge into clarity again—with a better understanding of what Genezisi builds.**

## The three acts

1. **ACT I — Discover the website:** Centered hero → page-derived interface enters → complete B composition → actual site geometry becomes depth.
2. **ACT II — Discover the engineering:** A planned process → one fictional enquiry → ordinary automation → connected systems → useful AI interpretation → permitted availability → prepared reply and an appropriate human approval gate.
3. **ACT III — Discover the studio:** Spatial structure folds forward into centered C → normal scrolling into actual Selected Work → clear individual services → working approach, studio and contact.

These are conceptual moments, **not individual compulsory screens**. Native scroll drives one continuous, reversible journey. No forced waiting, autoplay, fake backend, or scroll interception.

## ACT I — THE WEBSITE BECOMES THE WORLD

### 00 — The centered first impression

**What the visitor sees:** The genuine Genezisi brand, restrained warm paper background, generous negative space and a central headline. No right-hand interface card initially. Real navigation remains available on the first frame.

**Headline (retained):** “Your website is only the beginning.”  
**Proposed supporting copy:** “We build distinctive websites, dependable workflows and practical AI systems. Each works on its own—or together.”  
**Visible service identifiers:** Websites · Automation · AI systems.  
**Real controls:** Explore our work → real `#work`; Start a project → real contact; Services → real `#capabilities` or service anchor.  
**Subtle invitation:** “Scroll to see what lies behind the experience.”

**Meaning:** A polished studio that designs and engineers useful systems. The page still works commercially when motion is unavailable or skipped.

### 01 — The interface enters and makes room

**What moves (founder clarification, 29 October, superseding the earlier two-column finish):** On the visitor's first meaningful downward scroll, the structured interface enters from the right. The centered headline, supporting copy and actions move **completely out of the viewport to the left**, pushed by the arriving interface, rather than simply ending in the left column. Motion is driven by native scroll and must never let the two readable regions overlap.

**Physical logic:** The arriving panel creates room, like one part of a coherent editorial composition reorganizing to accommodate another. Add restrained depth and easing, not a literal collision or a generic carousel.

**Mobile:** The introduction moves fully upward out of view as the opaque interface rises into the center; do not compress the desktop layout. The introduction and its links must remain legible and accessible until they exit, then be excluded from focus order while offscreen; the persistent navigation remains usable.

**Meaning:** The visitor is already experiencing the transformation of a website before the journey starts.

### 02 — The full B composition resolves

**What the visitor sees:** One **large, centrally settled, fully opaque green Interface B** is the complete keyframe. The introductory headline has left the viewport; it is **not** parked in a left column. On large screens, the interface occupies generous space and all three service labels, heading, boundaries and supporting copy are clearly readable before any departure begins.

**Interface content:** “THREE DISCIPLINES”; one editorial focal phrase such as “Form and function, connected.”; distinct zones for “Websites”, “Automation”, and “AI systems”. Each can be commissioned separately. The interface is a graphical expression of our offer, **not** a fake external client dashboard.

**Rule:** No early opacity loss, crossing translucent text, or illegible intermediate state. This is a deliberately authored *complete* keyframe, not merely a frame in a fade.

### 03 — The visitor moves forward *through the real website*

**What happens:** The opening's original alignments and the **large centered interface frame** become spatial structure. The departed headline's grid ancestry—not ghost text or visible duplicate typography—can inform the left plane, while B's edges establish near/far structural elements and vanishing-point geometry. The site grid extends into depth; nearby surfaces pass the viewpoint while distant surfaces move less.

**Visual proof:** Actual occlusion, relative parallax and perspective from identifiable page elements. They retain enough material solidity that they do not turn into transparent overlapping posters. The camera follows a controlled forward axis; it does not simply enlarge a flat screenshot.

**Optional microcopy:** “Beyond the interface.”

**Meaning:** There is designed structure and engineering behind the experience. The site becomes the world, rather than opening a separate fantasy portal.

## ACT II — INSIDE A THOUGHTFULLY ENGINEERED SOLUTION

### 04 — A planned process is revealed (short connective beat)

**Central thought:** “First, we design how it should work.”  
**Support:** “Clear steps. Connected tools. Checks where they matter.”

**Visual:** The existing architecture reveals a simple, predefined route between receiving, organizing, checking and responding. Routes are shown as deliberate parts of a system—not improvised each time a message comes in. No dense technical flowchart or confusing miniature dashboards.

**Meaning:** Genezisi provides solution architecture and workflow/system engineering, not just plug-and-play AI. This is a brief introductory transition, not a long stand-alone screen.

### 05 — One believable enquiry arrives

**Heading:** “A new enquiry arrives.”  
**Canonical fictional message:** “Hi, I'd like to learn more about your services. Could we talk next week? Afternoons work best.”

**Visual:** The message appears at the stable central reading position on a plausible, legible surface. A nearby source/destination route activates; nothing claims to be a real Genezisi prospect or real customer data.

**Meaning:** Everyone recognizes the problem. This single message is the source for *every* later step.

### 06 — Ordinary automation checks and saves the request

**Heading:** “Your request is saved.”  
**Support:** “The details are checked and organized for your team.”

**Visual:** The same message travels through a visible validation boundary into a receiving record. The saved state changes only when the item arrives. A simple notification pathway may illuminate, but does not require a separate explanatory card.

**Meaning:** Repeatable, rule-based work can be automated reliably **without AI**. It can check required information, preserve the original request, prepare notifications and record a consistent outcome.

### 07 — Connected systems do their jobs

**Heading:** “Your tools work together.”  
**Support:** “Information moves where it's needed, without being copied by hand.”

**Visual:** A receiving workspace, a team workspace and a calendar-like destination exist as recognizable architectural structures connected by the previously revealed route. They are not invented unrelated apps. The original request remains traceable. Do not show every possible integration.

**Meaning:** Integration and system engineering are distinct from AI. The system has defined responsibilities, sensible information paths and safe boundaries. The user can commission this work without commissioning a website or AI.

### 08 — Your AI assistant understands

**Heading:** “Your AI assistant understands.”  
**Support:** “It recognizes what the person is asking for.”

**Visual:** The *same original message* remains readable. The phrases “next week” and “Afternoons work best” are highlighted and resolve into two straightforward visible preferences: “Next week” and “Afternoons.” No futuristic brain icon, fake stream of thoughts or jargon.

**Meaning:** The difference between automation and AI becomes obvious. Ordinary rules organized the information; AI interprets what the person meant.

### 09 — The assistant checks an authorized source

**Heading:** “Finding suitable times.”  
**Support (only if needed):** “Checking example availability.”

**Visual:** The assistant's extracted preferences travel along one traceable route to a calendar-like destination representing a **permitted read-only check**. Two example times return to the center: **Tuesday · 2:00 PM** and **Thursday · 3:00 PM**.

**Meaning:** An AI assistant can use a relevant, authorized tool to produce a useful answer, not merely chat. This is simulated data, not anyone's calendar or an actual booking.

### 10 — A practical reply takes shape

**Heading:** “Your reply is ready.”  
**Suggested reply:** “Would Tuesday at 2:00 PM or Thursday at 3:00 PM suit you?”

**Visual:** The same two returned times become part of a coherent reply on a single central readable surface. Avoid replacing the story with an unrelated new card; the source, options and draft are visually linked.

**Meaning:** The pieces of a system produce a practical result rather than a technology demonstration for its own sake.

### 11 — Automation with the right level of control

**Primary line (proposed):** “Automated when it makes sense. Approved when it matters.”  
**Short explanation:** “Routine steps can run automatically. Important actions can wait for you.”

**Visual:** Two quiet, comprehensible conceptual paths exist within the same designed system: `Runs automatically` and `Needs approval`. One may use a simple automatic-flow symbol and the other a person/review symbol. **In this specific example, the reply is on the Needs approval path.** The saved and organized request belonged to the routine automatic path.

**Final visible state:** **“Your reply is ready for review.”** / “You decide what happens next.” / discreet “Not sent.”

**Meaning:** We design automation around business rules and risk. A client may choose a fully automated path for suitable routine actions or human approval for consequential ones. We do not claim everything needs AI or every action must be approved.

**Truth rules:** No clickless ‘approved’, ‘sent’, ‘booked’ or ‘completed’ state. The act of scrolling is never consent or a real transaction. If a visible button looks clickable, it must have meaningful explicitly labelled demo-only behaviour; simplest version is a static, clear status instead.

**Discreet persistent demo disclosure throughout Acts II–III:** “Illustrative example — no live actions.” Accessible equivalent description required.

**Meaning:** The visual symmetry completes: center → left → depth → center. But the viewer understands more at the end than at the beginning.

**Work examples verified in the existing source:** TK Counsel, Frankencoin Desk, Her House Pilates. Preserve their actual titles, destinations, localized descriptions and project imagery; verify them in the current integration branch before final release.

### 16 — How Genezisi works (concise; fit into the current real site's information architecture)

A compact five-part explanation, not another elaborate scroll film:

Never promise “foolproof” or guarantee that AI never errs. Don't fabricate success rates, client logos, or audits.

### 17 — About the studio and direct contact

**Contact:** Clear “Start a project” route using the **existing verified contact channel**; navigation must work from the first frame and throughout the journey. A simple invitation such as “Tell us what you're trying to solve.” is preferable to a complex forced intake funnel.

**Footer:** Normal studio identity, legal/privacy links and verified contact/navigation links; preserve whatever legitimately exists in the site.

## Narrative rules that apply to every moment

### 1. One story, one source of truth

All later preferences, examples and the unsent reply derive from **one fictional general services enquiry**. No contradictory days, changing requirements or fake booked outcomes. Render central copy as accessible semantic DOM text, not a texture or video.

### 2. One central reading axis

The environment can have complex depth, but the important text appears in approximately the same comfortable central reading zone. One main piece of meaning at a time; no simultaneous stack of legible dashboards.

### 3. A logical connection between architecture and action

- The interface's **actual** borders and columns become spatial planes.
- A pre-designed route is visible **before** the request travels along it.
- A receiving surface changes to “saved” **after** the request arrives.
- AI highlights specific words **from the same source message**.
- A permitted check yields the two fixed example times.
- Those times visibly become the draft reply.
- The final review surface becomes a continuity anchor in the **forward** fold toward C.

Never rely on two unrelated panels crossfading to suggest a physical journey.

### 4. The environment is alive but not noisy

Use purposeful geometry, material solidity, receding edges, occlusion, restrained lighting and state changes that correspond to real conceptual actions. Avoid empty glowing hallways, decorative dashboards, random particle clutter and unnecessary extra scrolling.

### 5. Clear control boundaries

**Automatic in the example:** receiving, basic validation, saving/organizing, optional internal notification preparation. **AI in the example:** interpreting the time preference, checking a permitted illustrative availability source, drafting a suitable reply. **Human in the example:** reviewing and deciding whether to send the reply. Other commissioned processes can be fully automated when appropriate and explicitly authorized.

### 6. Practical interaction contract

- Browser-native scroll, forwards/backwards, at any speed; no scroll hijack or enforced pauses.
- Every partially completed state must still appear intentional and intelligible.
- Direct Work, Services and Contact links always usable; keyboard and skip links supported.
- On mobile, author a distinct *vertical* push and narrow-perspective composition. Do not shrink the desktop world until text becomes tiny.
- Reduced-motion and no-WebGL modes preserve complete meaning in a calm static/normal-scroll presentation; no-JavaScript mode remains a functional commercial homepage.
- English, Georgian and Dutch routes must be checked against the current site, with proper translation and typographic review before production.
- Keep site images/links real; no invented achievements or false live integrations.

### 7. Pacing contract

Do not convert the numbered moments into 18 full-screen cards. The cinematic story is one **compact continuous scene** with a few carefully authored readable states. Every scroll range must do one of three things: **communicate, reveal or meaningfully move**. A completed composition may settle briefly in scroll-distance terms, but no nearly-empty holding spans or fading a card before it is fully shown. Motion is derived from scroll progress, not clocks or autoplay.
