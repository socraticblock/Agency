# GENEZISI V2 — Creative & Experience Specification v0.1

**Status:** Creative direction locked enough to prototype; not yet a production implementation spec  
**Date:** 4 October 2026  
**Primary goal:** Build an unusually memorable studio website that makes the visitor think **“Whoa. I want something like this.”** and then makes it effortless to contact Genezisi.  
**Core funnel:** **Impress → Prove → Contact**  
**Primary conversion:** direct message / WhatsApp / phone contact  

---

## 1. North star

Genezisi V2 is not a complicated sales funnel and not an automation product demo disguised as a studio site.

Its job is simple:

1. **Impress** — demonstrate exceptional taste, motion, frontend craft and technical capability.
2. **Prove** — show enough real work and believable capability that the visual impression has substance.
3. **Contact** — let the visitor message the founder immediately, without a long form, sales call requirement or qualification flow.

The website itself is the strongest sales artifact.

The experience should communicate, mostly through execution rather than claims:

- Genezisi can design.
- Genezisi can build.
- Genezisi understands motion and interaction.
- Genezisi can create websites, AI systems and automations.
- Clients work directly with the person responsible for the work.

The visitor should never need to understand technical terms such as LLMs, RAG, webhooks or workflow orchestration before contacting Genezisi.

---

## 2. Product positioning

### What Genezisi is

**A small, high-end digital studio building websites, AI systems and automation.**

Do not over-index on the category label “digital systems studio” in public copy. It is useful internally, but the public-facing site should stay concrete.

### What Genezisi is not

- A cheap template website shop.
- A generic “AI agency.”
- A SaaS workflow platform.
- A fake large agency.
- A consultancy that forces a discovery call before a visitor can say hello.

### Positioning behavior

The site should show that websites, AI and automation can work together, but it must also make it clear that a visitor can hire Genezisi for **one useful thing**. A website-only project must still feel welcome.

---

## 3. Creative concept — THE SIGNAL

The signature Genezisi visual device is **The Signal**.

A Signal represents a real customer intention entering a business:

> “Can I book a consultation next Tuesday?”

The Signal is not a literal glowing orb everywhere. It is a repeatable brand behavior: a small turquoise active marker/pulse that identifies **something moving from request to useful result**.

### Signal grammar

**Receive → Route → Resolve → Review when needed**

- **Receive:** a new request becomes active.
- **Route:** the same request visibly moves through the necessary system.
- **Resolve:** the result aligns, settles and becomes still.
- **Review:** when human judgment is needed, the flow visibly branches to a named person/action instead of pretending automation solves everything.

### Why this works

The customer request—not the software—is the protagonist.

The visitor does not watch “Website → AI → CRM → Calendar.”
They watch one request become useful.

The technical system is revealed as the machinery behind that journey.

---

## 4. Brand feeling

The target feeling is:

**Dark. Precise. Cinematic. Technical. Editorial. Human. Confident.**

It should feel expensive without becoming cold or corporate.

### Keep from the current Genezisi identity

- Dark navy / near-black world.
- Turquoise/green accent.
- GENEZISI wordmark / letter spacing direction, subject to small-size polish.
- Bold typography.
- Founder-led tone.

### Change from the current site

- Stop relying on repeated card grids as the main layout language.
- Reduce visible borders.
- Remove “dashboard everywhere” visual language.
- Give large sections much more breathing room.
- Make projects much larger and more editorial.
- Replace generic service icons with type, imagery and motion.
- Treat the founder section as a real human moment rather than another product card.
- Remove pricing as a dominant homepage block for this version.
- Replace “Book a website call” as the primary action with direct messaging/contact.

---

## 5. Color rules

### Base

- Near-black navy background.
- White primary text.
- Cool gray secondary text.
- A very small number of surface levels.

### Turquoise rule

**Turquoise means activity.**

Use it for:

- The Signal.
- Active path.
- Selected state.
- Primary CTA.
- Confirmed connection / success.

Do **not** scatter turquoise around merely as decoration.

This semantic role makes the color feel intentional and gives Genezisi a recognizable interaction language.

---

## 6. Typography direction

Typography should do a significant portion of the art direction.

### Desired behavior

- Large, confident display type.
- High contrast between major statements and support copy.
- Tight, deliberate line lengths.
- Very readable body copy.
- Limited monospace only for small system/state labels if useful.

### Rough scale starting points

These are prototype values, not final CSS tokens.

**Desktop**
- Hero: ~72–112px responsive.
- Major section title: ~56–88px.
- Project title: ~40–64px.
- Body: ~17–19px.

**Mobile**
- Hero: ~42–58px depending on wording and viewport.
- Section title: ~36–48px.
- Project title: ~30–40px.
- Body: ~17–18px.

Georgian typography must be designed with real Georgian copy and equivalent visual hierarchy. Do not force English line breaks onto Georgian.

---

## 7. Navigation

Navigation should be calm and minimal.

### Desktop

Left: **GENEZISI**  
Center/right: **Work · What I Build · About**  
Primary action: **Message me**

The exact labels can change, but there should be very few destinations.

### Mobile

- GENEZISI at top left.
- Compact menu button if needed.
- **Message me** remains easy to reach.

### Persistent contact

After the hero begins, a compact **Message me** action may stay available in a corner / header.

It must feel integrated, not like a third-party sales widget.

# 8. Homepage structure

The homepage is deliberately simple:

1. **Immediate hero / proposition**
2. **Signature Signal sequence**
3. **Selected work**
4. **What Genezisi builds**
5. **Optional system/demo moment**
6. **Founder / Small by design**
7. **Direct contact**
8. **Minimal footer**

The homepage should not contain a giant process timeline, dense FAQ wall and three-column pricing table unless later evidence justifies them.

# 9. HERO — desktop storyboard

## Frame 0 — first paint

The first render must already look finished before any animation runs.

**Visible immediately:**

- Genezisi navigation.
- Very large headline.
- One concise service descriptor.
- A premium website interface / project-style composition.
- Message CTA.

### Copy territory

The exact headline remains open until visual composition testing. Preferred territories:

**Option A**  
# Your website is only the beginning.
Websites, AI and automation built around how your business works.

**Option B**  
# Websites. AI. Automation.
Built to work together.

**Option C**  
# Designed to be noticed. Built to be useful.
Websites and digital systems for real businesses.

The homepage should not rely on a vague slogan without an explicit descriptor.

---

## Frame 1 — enquiry arrives

A customer request enters the visible website UI:

> **Can I book a consultation next Tuesday?**

A small turquoise Signal appears with the request.

The motion is restrained. The point is recognition, not spectacle yet.

**Message:** someone gets in touch.

---

## Frame 2 — surface separates

As the visitor scrolls, the website surface shifts slightly and reveals an operational layer behind it.

This is the first true “whoa” moment.

Rules:

- Shallow perspective only.
- Readable text remains front-facing.
- No arbitrary 360° camera spin.
- No unreadable miniaturized interface.
- The reveal should feel physically elegant, not like a game HUD.

**Message:** a beautiful website can be the front of something smarter.

---

## Frame 3 — Signal routes

The same Signal travels into the next step.

The system checks relevant business information and/or availability.

Example states:

- Request received.
- Availability checked.
- Customer details structured.

The path should be visibly finite and easy to follow.

**Message:** the system understands what needs to happen next.

---

## Frame 4 — useful result

The Signal reaches the booking/record state.

The interface displays a believable result:

**Consultation request**  
Tuesday · 14:00  
Customer details  
Status: ready / confirmed depending on scenario

Never imply that a booking was created if the illustrative flow has not shown the availability step.

---

## Frame 5 — human judgment

A secondary path demonstrates restraint:

**Needs review → Socratic / Owner**

This should be visually distinct from the automated route.

**Message:** automation handles repetition; people stay in control where judgment matters.

---

## Frame 6 — completion as stillness

The scene pulls back just enough to show the complete relationship.

Then movement stops.

The panels align.
The Signal settles.
The active glow quiets.

No confetti.
No huge success burst.

**Completion is calm.**

Possible concluding line:

# Your website was only the beginning.

Secondary action:

**See the work ↓**

The scene releases back into ordinary scrolling.

---

## Desktop hero behavior rules

- One bounded pinned scene only.
- Normal navigation remains outside the pinned animation.
- A visitor can scroll quickly past it.
- Backward scroll reverses the visual story only; no external action is ever performed.
- A skip / work route should be available if needed.
- The scene is illustrative unless explicitly connected to a real safe demo.
- No freeform AI input in V2 launch.
- No live booking is required for the experience to work.

---

# 10. HERO — mobile storyboard

**Mobile is not a compressed desktop hero.**

The story is re-authored vertically.

## Mobile chapter 1 — Receive

Full-width / near-full-screen website view.

Large text above or integrated with composition.

Customer enquiry appears.

The turquoise Signal activates.

No long pin.

---

## Mobile chapter 2 — Route

As the visitor scrolls, the Signal visually travels downward into the next chapter.

Only one principal interface is shown at a time.

The system state changes:

**Checking availability…**  
**Request recorded.**

A short connector or line can visually bridge chapters.

---

## Mobile chapter 3 — Resolve

The owner notification / result becomes the hero object.

The Signal arrives and settles.

A small optional human-review branch is visible.

Then:

# Your website was only the beginning.

**See the work ↓**

---

## Mobile wow factor

Mobile should feel premium through:

- Full-screen typography.
- Precise vertical pacing.
- Edge-to-edge project imagery.
- One primary object per screen.
- Signal continuity.
- Clean state morphs.
- Strong type/image transitions.
- Deliberate stillness between motion moments.

Do not use:

- Tiny desktop workflow maps.
- Hover-dependent content.
- Long scroll-jacked pinning.
- Horizontal drag as the only path forward.
- Tilt-dependent text.
- Persistent heavy WebGL just to mimic desktop.

---

# 11. Motion system

Motion should feel authored as part of the brand.

## Core motion grammar

### Receive
- Localized emphasis.
- Fast acknowledgement.
- Signal appears.

### Route
- A single request marker moves.
- Connectors draw only when needed.
- Surfaces shift enough to reveal relationship, not to show off transforms.

### Resolve
- Layout becomes cleaner.
- Motion slows.
- Active state becomes stable.
- Success is represented by alignment and stillness.

### Review
- Route branches deliberately.
- Human state is named.
- The visitor can see that judgment has been preserved.

## Timing starting points

Prototype ranges only:

- Input acknowledgement: ~120–180ms.
- Small UI transition: ~200–300ms.
- Panel transition: ~300–450ms.
- Large scene motion: scroll-linked rather than fixed-duration where appropriate.

Never delay controls merely to finish an animation.

# 12. Selected work

After the signature sequence, the site becomes calmer.

This section should be highly editorial, not a wall of equal cards.

## Presentation

Feature approximately **2–4 strong projects**.

Each project receives:

- Large visual.
- Project name.
- Category.
- One concise statement of what was built.
- Optional short “problem / build / result or status” annotation.
- Clear route to view it / case study.

### Trust labels

Every project/example is labeled accurately as one of:

- **Client project**
- **Internal system**
- **Prototype**
- **Illustrative demo**

Never present a demo as a client result.

### Existing current-site work

The current site visually includes examples such as TK Counsel, Frankencoin Desk, Her House Pilates and Sakartvelo Defenders. Before V2 launch, verify which are real/live, which have permission to show, and which are placeholders. Only verified work should remain.

### Case-study structure

For real case studies:

**Problem → What I built → What changed / What shipped**

If outcomes were not measured, say what shipped rather than inventing business metrics.

---

# 13. What Genezisi builds

Do not use three generic cards as the main presentation.

Use a concise editorial section:

## Websites
Beautiful, fast digital experiences that make the business look credible and make the next action obvious.

## AI systems
Useful assistants and tools that can answer, retrieve information, prepare work or help handle repetitive requests.

## Automation
Connections between the tools a business already uses so repeated tasks do not need to be copied, chased and re-entered manually.

Keep the language concrete.

Avoid technical jargon unless the visitor chooses to read implementation detail.

---

# 14. Optional capability demo

If we want a second interactive moment after selected work, it must be smaller than the hero.

Preferred format:

**Project in three views**

- Customer view.
- Business/owner view.
- Behind-the-scenes implementation view.

Use the same underlying example so the visitor understands the relationship between presentation and system behavior.

Do not add a freeform “build your own workflow” playground to V2 launch.

---

# 15. Founder section — Small by design

This should be the main human counterpoint to the technical visual world.

## Recommended structure

# Small by design.

A real founder portrait.

Copy should be personal and operationally true:

> You work directly with me—the person designing and building the project. No account-manager telephone game and no mystery handoff.

Use **I** when the founder is speaking personally. Do not maintain fake agency plural if the visitor is actually dealing with one person.

Possible supporting points:

- Direct communication.
- Custom work.
- Clear handover.
- Support after launch.

Keep it short.

---

# 16. Pricing / investment decision

For the current goal, **remove the large pricing-card section from the homepage**.

The user’s desired behavior is: see the work → want it → message → discuss the project.

Therefore V2 does not need to force every visitor into predefined packages.

Possible later options:

- A subtle “Website projects from ₾999” note if qualification becomes necessary.
- A separate investment page.
- No public price at all for bespoke systems.

If pricing is published later, AI/system quotes must separate:

**implementation + Genezisi support + third-party running costs.**

No vague “unlimited AI” pricing.

---

# 17. Contact experience

This is intentionally simple.

## Persistent action

**Message me**

Link to the owner’s chosen real contact route (ideally WhatsApp if that is the preferred channel).

## Final CTA

Large final section:

# Want something like this?

**Send me a message.**

Show the real contact method prominently.

Possible supporting line:

> Tell me what you want to build. We’ll go from there.

No mandatory budget field.
No compulsory discovery call.
No AI bot gate.
No ten-question questionnaire.

If a small web form exists as an alternative, it should ask only for:

- Message.
- Reply address / phone.
- Optional name.

The direct number/message action remains visible.

---

# 18. Footer

Minimal:

- GENEZISI.
- Contact / number.
- Selected social/profile routes if actually used.
- Privacy/legal only where required.
- Language switch if Georgian/English is supported.

No giant sitemap is needed for the first version.

# 19. Interaction shortlist for V2 launch

Ship a small number at a very high level.

## Core

1. **Persistent Signal** — same request identity through the hero.
2. **Surface → System reveal** — customer-facing website reveals the operational layer behind it.
3. **Human-review branch** — one request visibly routes to a person.
4. **Completion as stillness** — success settles instead of celebrating noisily.

## Supporting

5. **Question → contact context** — a project/service example can prefill or carry context toward the contact action.
6. **Project in three views** — customer / business / behind-the-scenes for the strongest system example.

Everything else waits unless prototype testing proves it materially improves the experience.

---

# 20. Mobile hard gates

No desktop section is approved until its mobile equivalent is defined.

Every mobile section must pass:

- Essential content fits without zooming.
- No critical hover dependency.
- No accidental horizontal overflow.
- Primary controls are comfortably tappable (~44px target minimum).
- Sticky UI does not obstruct browser controls or on-screen keyboard.
- Project imagery is intentionally cropped for portrait, not merely shrunk.
- Motion remains understandable during fast scroll.
- Reduced-motion version is complete.
- Contact action is reachable with one thumb.

---

# 21. Performance gates

A premium technical studio cannot ship a visually impressive but laggy site.

## Targets

- Core Web Vitals target: LCP ≤ 2.5s, INP ≤ 200ms, CLS ≤ 0.1 at the 75th percentile.
- Aim for roughly 60fps on representative devices where feasible.
- Avoid a hero video as a prerequisite for first paint.
- Initial visible mobile assets should remain deliberately constrained; prototype budget around ~1MB before tuning.
- Scene code must be separated from core page behavior.
- No perpetual render loop when scene is out of view or tab is hidden.
- Images have responsive sizes and reserved dimensions.
- Fonts are subset carefully, including Georgian glyph needs if Georgian is supported.
- WebGL, if used, gets a static fallback and reduced fidelity on weaker devices.

These are validation gates, not framework guarantees.

---

# 22. Accessibility / resilience

The site must remain good when effects are unavailable.

### Reduced motion

Provide a designed static composition and short explanatory states.

No long pinning, traveling signals or perspective motion.

### No JavaScript / animation failure

The visitor must still see:

- What Genezisi does.
- Selected work.
- Founder.
- Contact information.

The business must not disappear when the animation fails.

### Keyboard / focus

- Navigation and contact controls work from keyboard.
- Scrolling never steals focus.
- Decorative duplicate text is hidden from assistive technology.
- Color is not the only indicator of status.

---

# 23. Technical direction — not yet a stack lock

The design should be technically achievable with a lightweight baseline.

### Preferred first prototype

- Existing repo/framework if healthy.
- GSAP + ScrollTrigger for the hero timeline.
- DOM for readable UI.
- SVG for Signal paths/connectors.
- CSS transform/opacity for ordinary motion.

### Conditional tools

- React only where state complexity warrants it.
- Three.js / React Three Fiber only if a visual prototype is materially better than DOM/SVG.
- WebGL shader only as an isolated enhancement.
- Smooth-scroll library only if testing proves a specific advantage.

Do not rewrite the site into Astro/Next/anything else until the existing repository has been audited.

# 25. Implementation sequence

## Phase 1 — Creative lock

- Approve headline territory.
- Approve Signal visual behavior.
- Choose hero demo scenario.
- Approve homepage section order.
- Decide public languages.
- Decide contact destination.

## Phase 2 — Static storyboard

Create desktop and mobile static keyframes for:

- First paint.
- Enquiry received.
- Surface/System reveal.
- Routing state.
- Human-review state.
- Final settled state.
- First selected-work section.

If these frames do not look premium without animation, do not animate them yet.

## Phase 3 — Isolated hero prototype

Build only the signature scene.

Test:

- Visual impact.
- Comprehension.
- Fast scroll.
- Reverse scroll.
- Resize.
- Desktop performance.
- Real iPhone Safari.
- Real Android Chrome, including a mid-range device.
- Reduced motion.

Do not build the entire site until the hero reaches the desired standard.

## Phase 4 — Design system and quiet sections

- Typography.
- Spacing.
- Project layout.
- Founder section.
- Contact treatment.
- Navigation.

## Phase 5 — Full build

Integrate the approved hero into the site and build the remaining sections.

## Phase 6 — Obsessive polish

- Easing.
- Spacing.
- Crop decisions.
- Line breaks.
- Tap behavior.
- Loading.
- Motion timing.
- Browser differences.
- Keyboard behavior.
- Contact reliability.

## Phase 7 — Launch QA

Ship only after visual, performance, mobile and contact gates pass.

# 26. Acceptance criteria

Genezisi V2 is ready when all of the following are true.

## Impression

- The first screen looks premium before motion begins.
- The hero contains at least one genuinely memorable transition/reveal.
- The experience still feels premium in a screenshot.
- The site does not resemble a generic automation SaaS template.

## Understanding

After a few seconds, a visitor can tell that Genezisi builds:

- Websites.
- AI systems/assistants.
- Automation.

without needing technical knowledge.

## Proof

- Real work is visible immediately after the signature experience.
- Placeholders are removed.
- Demos/prototypes are labeled honestly.

## Contact

- A visitor can message the owner quickly from desktop and mobile.
- No required sales call or long form stands in the way.
- The number / WhatsApp destination is correct and tested.

## Mobile

- The mobile experience is intentionally authored.
- No essential interaction depends on hover.
- The hero remains smooth and understandable.
- Contact is comfortably reachable with one thumb.

## Technical

- Reduced-motion version is complete.
- No-JS/failed-animation baseline still explains the business.
- Real-device testing passes.
- Core Web Vitals are within the agreed launch thresholds or any exception is explicitly accepted.

# 27. What we deliberately are NOT building in V2 launch

- A full workflow builder.
- A live general-purpose AI chatbot in the hero.
- A complex lead qualification wizard.
- A giant package/pricing comparison grid.
- A huge FAQ.
- A custom cursor required for navigation.
- Constant background animation.
- A 3D scene simply because 3D is available.
- Fake statistics or fake client outcomes.
- Fake agency headcount.
- An experience that only works well on desktop.
