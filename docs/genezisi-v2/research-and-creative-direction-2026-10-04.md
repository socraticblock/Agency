# Genezisi V2 — Research and creative direction

Prepared 4 October 2026 · Strategy and design research · No production implementation

## Reading this report

The supplied handoff is the source for Genezisi’s current positioning, colors and approximate ₾999 / ₾1,999 / ₾3,999 packages. I have not independently audited the current Genezisi site, its source code, analytics, project profitability or screenshot. Those descriptions remain brief inputs, not verified site findings.

Research used current search retrieval and opened official websites, studio case studies and technical documentation. It establishes public offers, content structure, examples and documented capabilities. It does **not** constitute a hands-on browser motion audit: camera paths, hover behavior, mobile frame rates and conversion rates were not measured. Study recommendations below identify what to investigate and adapt; they do not claim that every suggested effect is currently running on the linked site. Inaccessible pages were excluded from the evidence base. Sources are linked in the final register, with references beside factual claims.

**Evidence** means a public statement or documented capability. **Interpretation** means a conclusion drawn from this limited sample. **Recommendation** means proposed Genezisi design or commercial behavior. Competitor delivery and outcome claims are self-reported, not independently verified. This is a useful decision brief, not a comprehensive market census or an awards ranking.

## A. Executive conclusion

**Build a studio website that lets a business owner understand and explore what happens after a customer gets in touch.**

The strongest positioning is:

> Genezisi designs and builds websites, AI assistants and workflows for service businesses—from the first enquiry to the next action.

Recommended creative headline:

> **A better first impression. A better next step.**

Supporting copy:

> Custom websites, useful AI and connected workflows. Built around how your business actually works.

This is a recommendation, not a tested winner. It combines visual craft with a comprehensible business promise and avoids suggesting that automation guarantees sales. Start with service businesses that handle enquiries, appointments and follow-ups; expand the narrative once real project evidence supports more sectors. The brief names no proven niche, so this is a proposed initial focus, not an established customer profile.

### What research changes

The three-capability proposition is already present nearby. ALLONE in Tbilisi publicly combines websites, AI agents and systems, while emphasizing direct contact and small size [S01]. Weblier combines websites, software and AI automation [S02]. Nimbus combines websites, chatbots and AI employees [S03]. Arevian in Yerevan connects design, engineering, AI and business operations [S06].

**Interpretation:** “Digital systems studio” is a useful category label, but weak as the entire differentiation. “We build your business’s digital side” also has close regional parallels. Avoid treating either wording as an original market position.

The differentiator should be the combination of:

1. Distinctive visual craft, visible immediately.
2. One understandable working example, rather than an abstract network.
3. Real project evidence and clear labels for demonstrations.
4. Direct access to the builder and a bounded first project.
5. Clear operating costs, ownership and post-launch support.

**Keep the connected-system concept. Change its protagonist from software nodes to one customer request.** The request stays visually consistent as the website, assistant and workflow process it. The owner receives a useful result—and remains in control of exceptions.

Do not require 30–60 seconds of scrolling before visitors see proof, pricing or contact. The experience can reward that amount of exploration; it should reveal its central meaning much sooner.

### Crowded language and possible whitespace

**Evidence:** integrated services, direct contact, AI, speed and business systems recur in the inspected sample.

**Interpretation:** crowded language includes “all under one roof,” “AI-native,” “digital transformation,” and broad promises to build everything. These words do little to help an owner identify a first purchase.

**Proposed whitespace:** a visibly accomplished studio that explains a small useful system in plain language, publishes the boundaries of the first step, and shows both automation and human judgment. This is a hypothesis to test with prospects, not proof of an unserved market.

The commercial choice is not only between agencies: it also includes keeping the existing site, handling enquiries manually, hiring a freelancer, using a template or doing nothing. The homepage must make a manageable improvement feel more attractive than postponement.

### What feels durable, and what risks becoming a cliché

This is a design judgment informed by the references, not a statistical survey of 2026 design.

| Technique | Recommendation for Genezisi | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Large editorial typography | Use for a short headline, project titles and section rhythm | Creates hierarchy without an expensive render loop. |
| Kinetic / variable typography | Limit to a deliberate title transition, if readable in both languages | Text motion should change emphasis, not postpone reading. Verify font glyph coverage and licensing before selection. |
| Dark surfaces and turquoise | Retain, but let color identify active states | The palette is an existing brand input. Functional color gives it a role beyond atmosphere. |
| Restrained glow and gradients | One small active-signal halo; optional subtle depth gradient | Blanket glow makes every element equally important. |
| Grids and asymmetry | Precise baseline grid; occasional offset project image | Order supports the systems proposition; controlled asymmetry supplies personality. |
| Cinematic transitions / scroll-linked UI | One compact signature sequence | Repetition across every section weakens emphasis and increases maintenance. |
| Animated diagrams / procedural visuals | Prefer one request traveling through a finite system | Shows causality and can remain comprehensible as a still image. |
| Spatial / mixed 2D–3D UI | Consider shallow perspective with readable DOM panels first | Depth should reveal relationships. Full 3D is optional. |
| Shader and WebGL effects | Use only if a prototype proves a meaningful visual advantage | Spectacle carries download, device and debugging costs. |
| Hover interactions / cursor effects | Add secondary detail on hover and equivalent focus/tap behavior | Do not conceal essential content behind a mouse-only interaction. |
| Minimal navigation | Work · What we build · Investment · Contact; visible language control | Minimal should mean fewer decisions, not hidden destinations. |

Avoid interchangeable dark dashboards, floating cubes, purple AI clouds, oversized decorative cursors, perpetual marquees and long preloaders. These are creative exclusions for this project, not claims that those techniques never work.

## D. Three genuinely different creative directions

### Direction 1 — The Next Step · recommended

**Central idea:** follow one customer enquiry from a beautiful website into a useful business action. A single request becomes the visual continuity across the experience.

**Visual language:** near-black editorial canvas, large white type, turquoise activity, shallow dimensional UI planes and a tightly framed workflow. Real founder photography breaks the technical atmosphere lower down.

**Hero:** large statement on one side; a composed sample service-business site on the other. An enquiry arrives. As visitors scroll, the interface reveals the task behind it.

**Motion:** receive → route → resolve. One pulse identifies the request; a line traces its path; a readable outcome settles. Human-review states have a distinct label and shape, not merely a different color.

**Homepage:** proposition → compact story → real work → three practical starting points → founder/process → investment → contact.

**Type:** assertive sans display plus calm text sans; a limited monospace for figure numbers. Georgian typography must be designed independently, with equivalent visual hierarchy rather than identical line breaks.

**Interaction:** optional alternate scenario and inspectable steps. Normal scroll remains sufficient. No game-like controls required.

**Advantages:** demonstrates the expanded offer; naturally leads to a business conversation; one consistent metaphor can carry the brand.

**Disadvantages:** generic nodes could resemble an automation platform; an unclear or fictitious scenario could reduce trust. Needs excellent sample UI and careful narrative editing.

**Difficulty / performance:** medium with DOM/SVG; high if upgraded to fully spatial WebGL. Good performance is feasible when the story is a bounded enhancement and content renders independently.

### Direction 2 — The Working Studio

**Central idea:** invite visitors into a founder’s working practice. The strongest proof is seeing decisions, prototypes and finished work.

**Visual language:** editorial portfolio with charcoal and occasional pale-paper sections, generous typography, real desk/process photography and annotated screens. Warmth comes from the person and the work, not simulated dashboards.

**Hero:** a short manifesto alongside one selected project with a visible “problem / built / current status” annotation. A slim founder introduction appears early.

**Motion:** sketch → test → refine. Short overlays align a wireframe with the shipped interface; annotations enter in a measured sequence; project transitions preserve image position.

**Homepage:** selected project → studio proposition → two more case studies → capabilities within those projects → working process → founder → investment/contact.

**Type:** editorial grotesk; optional restrained serif for founder quotations, subject to Georgian support. Layout supplies the premium quality.

**Interaction:** compare a prototype with a shipped state; inspect a design decision; open a narrated walkthrough.

**Advantages:** honest, human and proof-rich; lower technical risk; strong fit for a small studio with substantial existing work.

**Disadvantages:** depends heavily on quality photography and documented case studies. The AI/automation expansion may remain less memorable without a strong system project.

**Difficulty / performance:** low-to-medium engineering, high editorial effort. Strong baseline mobile performance; lazily loaded video must not displace the main content.

### Direction 3 — One Business Day

**Central idea:** show how a working day changes when repeated handoffs are connected. Time, not infrastructure, is the organizing metaphor.

**Visual language:** oversized clock notation, a vertical timeline, human messages and a few task surfaces. Dark background with distinct daylight chapters; fewer floating interfaces.

**Hero:** “Make room for the work that needs you.” A sample day starts with an incoming request, followed by a booking, an update and a flagged exception.

**Motion:** interrupt → organize → clear. Task fragments consolidate into a timeline; completed items move aside; an exception remains for a person.

**Homepage:** day narrative → alternate business-day scenario → work that shipped → choose one repetitive task → founder → scope/pricing → message.

**Type:** large numerals, disciplined sans headings, highly readable message text. Avoid fake live timestamps suggesting real client activity.

**Interaction:** select a job type; compare manual and connected handling; optionally estimate repeated-task time from visitor-supplied values.

**Advantages:** emotional and understandable; starts with the owner’s experience; distinct from a generic automation canvas.

**Disadvantages:** time-saving claims need evidence; website design could become visually secondary; a long day story could become repetitive.

**Difficulty / performance:** medium. DOM-based timeline works well on phones. A calculator increases logic and validation requirements.

### Decision

Choose **The Next Step**, borrow the founder warmth and annotated proof of **The Working Studio**, and use the day/time metaphor only within a relevant case study. Do not merge all three narratives into the homepage.

Direction 1 best fits the brief’s expanded offer and desired scroll experience. Direction 2 becomes preferable if the available project evidence is much stronger than the available system demonstrations.

## F. Hero and first scrolling sequence

### Scenario and continuity

Use a clearly labeled **illustrative demo for an appointment-based service business**. Example customer input: “Can I book a consultation next Tuesday?” The demo must show a requested slot being checked, not an assistant unconditionally promising availability.

Keep one request marker throughout. A short message fragment and persistent request ID tie the website input to the answer, record and notification. Use only four principal panels: website, assistant, booking/record, owner. Follow-up is a compact final state; payment is omitted unless it is necessary to explain that particular business.

### Desktop choreography

Prototype target: one scene container around 300–360vh tall, including approximately 200–260vh of scroll travel after pinning begins. Exact dimensions depend on viewport and copy. Measure and adjust after comprehension testing. The percentages below are progress through that scene, **not mandatory elapsed time**.

| Progress | Composition and movement | Copy / state | Controls and release behavior |
|---|---|---|---|
| Before scene | Clear headline, real navigation, site panel composed as part of the hero | Service descriptor and primary CTA visible | Work and contact available immediately. No preloader. |
| 0–15% · arrival | Sample enquiry appears in a form/chat panel. One small turquoise marker identifies it. | “Someone gets in touch.” Demo label remains visible. | Stable scene frame; continue or skip to work. |
| 15–35% · reveal | Site panel shifts left and gains shallow perspective, around 6–8 degrees at most. A second plane appears behind it. Text panels stay substantially front-facing. | “Give them a useful next step.” Assistant asks or answers from sample business information. | Main headline yields to a short chapter label. Navigation remains outside the animation. |
| 35–60% · action | Connection line draws toward booking/record panel. Request marker travels; relevant slot and record status become readable. | “Availability checked. Request recorded.” Customer confirmation is a separate state. | Scroll scrubs composition and progress; it does not send a real message or create a real booking. |
| 60–80% · owner | Layout widens to include a concise owner notification. One secondary branch can indicate human review. | “The right information reaches you.” | An optional “When it needs a person” button opens the exception example. |
| 80–100% · settle | All four panels align into a simple complete view. Perspective reduces and movement ends. | “A better first impression. A better next step.” | Scene releases into normal work content. “See what we’ve built” appears. |

Do not literally rotate readable text away from the visitor to achieve the peel effect. Animate surfaces and spatial relationships while keeping the current message legible.

### Timing, camera and input rules

There is no compulsory camera travel time. A normal exploratory visitor might spend 15–30 seconds here and another 15–30 exploring a variation. A fast visitor can scroll directly past it.

Use shallow depth, a single broad reveal and one concluding pullback. Prefer composition transforms to a continuously orbiting camera. Avoid a full-screen blur between chapters. The scene frame pins, while child planes move; the navigation and skip control remain independent. GSAP documents scrubbed timelines and pinning, and cautions against animating the pinned element itself [S31].

Start the motion prototype with input feedback around 120–180ms, panel transitions around 300–450ms, and connection confirmations around 200–300ms. These are art-direction starting values, not research-established optimal timings. Scroll-linked movement should map clearly to progress without excessive catch-up. Do not delay controls until transitions finish.

Scrolling backward reverses the illustrative visual sequence, but never external actions. The demo is a finite, local state model. Keyboard focus should not be moved by scrolling. Essential story text exists in meaningful document order; decorative duplicates are hidden from assistive technology.

### Optional interaction

After the main story, offer “Try another request” with two predefined scenarios: a straightforward booking and a request needing human review. The second resolves into a readable handoff, not a fake AI failure countdown. Free typing is a later enhancement, after the canned scenarios communicate well.

If free input is eventually introduced, label whether output is simulated or actually generated. Show “Example only—no message or booking is sent.” A live model requires bounded knowledge, usage limits, timeout handling and a reliable static example. It should never be necessary for the main proposition to load.

### Mobile

Use the same narrative in three ordinary stacked chapters: enquiry; reply and checked next step; owner notification. Show a single primary interface per chapter. No long pin, miniature network, horizontal drag dependency or tilt-dependent text.

Buttons can swap two example scenarios. Keep the work and contact anchors visible in the navigation. Make tap targets at least 44px as a project design target. Protect the form from sticky elements and the on-screen keyboard. At intermediate widths, choose the layout based on usable panel space rather than the device label alone.

### Reduced motion and no JavaScript

Reduced motion: a static final composition plus the three short explanatory chapters, without perspective, traveling signals or long pinning. The preference is detected through `prefers-reduced-motion`, documented by MDN [S33]. It is an alternate composition, not an empty hero.

Without JavaScript: the proposition, work, prices, contact routes and ordered demo explanation still render. If scripting is needed to submit the enhanced form, provide a conventional server-backed submission or a direct email alternative. Do not make the business disappear when the animation fails.

## G. Visual identity evolution

Current-design statements here come from the brief. Confirm against the actual brand files before production.

| Element | Retain / change | Proposed rule |
|---|---|---|
| Logo | Retain the established identity provisionally | Improve spacing and small-size rendering before considering a redesign. The mark need not carry the entire system metaphor. |
| Turquoise | Retain with a defined semantic role | Reserved for active path, selected control and primary action. Confirm contrast against actual background and text sizes. |
| Dark background | Retain with fewer arbitrary surface shades | Prototype near-black navy; lighter surface only where layer separation is needed. |

| Type scale | Increase contrast between major and supporting text | Desktop headline roughly 72–112px; mobile roughly 40–56px; body around 17–19px. Use responsive limits rather than fixed sizes everywhere. |
| Spacing | Increase major-section breathing room | Start with 96–160px desktop section spacing and 56–88px mobile; adjust to the content. |
| Iconography | Simplify and unify | One line system; text labels remain primary. AI is a capability, not a robot-head icon. |
| Borders and cards | Reduce | Border only where it clarifies a panel or control. Use editorial project sections instead of a wall of equal cards. |
| Glow / gradients | Restrict | Small signal halo and subtle depth treatment; no ambient glowing background competing with the work. |
| Texture | Optional, subtle and static | Texture must survive compression and not reduce small-text clarity. Avoid animated noise shaders by default. |
| Imagery | Make real work and founder photography dominant | Use high-quality screenshots with captions and context. No invented client screens presented as commissioned work. |
| 3D | Conditional | The shallow UI reveal is the first test. Full spatial rendering only if it materially improves the story. |
| Diagrams | Establish a proprietary grammar | Consistent connector radius, arrow logic, request marker and labeled states. The diagram should make sense without animation. |

### Motion as brand identity

Use a consistent **receive → route → resolve** grammar:

- Receive: one localized emphasis identifies new input.
- Route: a single visible path carries the same request marker.
- Resolve: the output aligns and becomes still, with a readable status.
- Review: a branching path lands on a named person/action state rather than pretending every request resolves automatically.

Apply this to the signature sequence, a project diagram and the real form’s status feedback. Do not force it onto every heading. Keep idle motion off by default; run motion only when useful and in view. The premium feeling should persist in a screenshot.

These are proposed copy territories, not conversion-tested claims. Final Georgian copy needs native editorial review and equivalent meaning, not a literal English line-length translation.

| Territory | Headline | Supporting direction | Evaluation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Experience plus operations · recommended | **A better first impression. A better next step.** | Websites, AI assistants and workflows built around your business. | Fits the visual reveal while allowing a website-only project. Needs an explicit service descriptor beneath it. |
| Plain service clarity | **Your website. Your enquiries. Connected.** | Design the experience, answer repeat questions and organize what happens next. | Direct and understandable; weaker for non-enquiry systems. |
| Ownership of a problem | **Less chasing. More doing.** | Connect the repeated steps between customer contact and your team. | Good for automation detail pages; too vague alone for the studio homepage. |

| Specific business task | **From “Can I book?” to a clear next step.** | A website, an assistant and a workflow working together. | Excellent demo chapter title; too narrow as the whole studio identity. |

Recommended primary CTA: **Tell us what you need**. Secondary: **See the work**. Within the demo: **See what happens next**. Within service pages: **Ask about this**. Form submit: **Send your message**.

“Tell us what’s slowing your business down” works within the automation section, but makes a new website enquiry sound like a diagnostic exercise. “Talk directly with the person building it” belongs near the founder/contact area.

Avoid “AI that talks to them” as the main AI explanation. It emphasizes conversation rather than usefulness. Explain repeat questions, information retrieval, task preparation and handoff instead. Do not promise that the system will always qualify, close or sell.

## I. Portfolio and trust strategy

### Case-study structure

Present **Problem → What we built → What changed**, with evidence appropriate to the project:

1. Context: business type, situation and constraints.
2. Problem: the actual friction the client described.
3. Build: website, assistant or workflow; show Genezisi’s exact responsibility.
4. Walkthrough: one task with input, action, output and exception.
5. Evidence: real screenshots, approved transcript samples, verified process changes or measured results.
6. Status: live, pilot, prototype or retired; date if relevant.

When results were not measured, say what shipped. “Enquiries are now recorded in one place” is valid when verified. A revenue uplift or hours saved is not valid without a measurement basis.

Label examples consistently: **Client project**, **Internal system**, **Prototype**, or **Illustrative demo**. Remove placeholder portfolio items from the launch version. One strong real case study and a clearly labeled internal demonstration are more useful than six ambiguous projects.

For website-only projects, show the design challenge, content structure and actual functionality. Do not redraw every old website as an integrated AI system it never was. For automation, use redacted records or recreated demonstration data labeled as such. If third parties contributed, credit them and describe the studio’s contribution.

### Founder-led credibility

Designjoy explicitly describes direct delivery by its sole founder [S27]. ALLONE likewise emphasizes small size and direct communication [S01]. These examples establish that smallness can be stated openly; they do not prove that a founder label alone improves enquiries.

Recommended treatment: real portrait, actual name, concise role and concrete collaboration promise. “You speak directly with the person designing and building your project” should appear only if operationally accurate. Avoid artificial staff biographies or inflated capacity.

Trust should come from inspectable work, clear scope, working previews, project ownership and precise support terms. Publish testimonials only with permission and retain their context. Publish response-time and availability claims only when they can be maintained.

## J. Pricing and commercial strategy

**Recommendation: do not lower all three prices now. Narrow the first purchase and make asking easier.**

The sampled public prices range from a ₾499 promotion at Webuild to website starting prices around ₾2,000 at aiWEB and Weblier [S02, S05, S07]. This is a scope-diverse sample, not evidence that Genezisi should match either end. Existing prices in the brief already place its smallest offering below those two higher starting points.

Premium presentation and accessible entry pricing can coexist if the low-cost scope is honest. They conflict when the site implies unlimited bespoke craft at the minimum price.

### Public pricing architecture

| Offer | Public presentation | Scope and cost rules |
|---|---|---|
| Focused website | A starting price tied to a named scope | Consider retaining ₾999 only if a small, repeatable build is profitable. Define pages, content responsibility, revisions, language and integrations. Do not promise signature 3D at that price. |
| Bespoke website | Starting scope or “priced after a short brief” | Separate complex content, ecommerce, booking and extensive motion. Existing ₾1,999/₾3,999 packages require a scope/margin review before republishing. |
| One workflow | Fixed quote after the task and integrations are understood | Define trigger, connected tools, volume, exception handling, testing and handover. Do not invent a starting price before costing representative projects. |
| AI assistant | Setup quote plus estimated operating costs | Define knowledge sources, channels, languages, handoff, usage assumptions and model/vendor dependencies. |
| Connected system | Phased proposal | Start with one bounded stage; specify later stages separately. Avoid asking the owner to fund an entire transformation upfront. |
| Care / support | Separate recurring service with named coverage | State monitoring, updates, support windows, included changes and exclusions. Keep provider usage fees visibly distinct. |

Use a compact homepage investment block; expand detail on service pages or a quote. Avoid three nearly identical metallic pricing cards implying that all work can be sorted by feature count.

### What a quote must separate

**Implementation + ongoing studio support + third-party running costs.**

Operating costs may include hosting, database/storage, email/messages, workflow platform, AI input/output usage, retrieval services and monitoring. Estimate these from a defined workload; distinguish a fixed support fee from consumption. Include an alert/cap policy and what the system does if the cap or provider outage is reached.

Use the model:

> Expected monthly cost = fixed platform costs + workload-dependent usage + agreed support fee.

A quote can show low/expected/high usage scenarios with the actual current provider prices verified at quoting time. This report intentionally supplies no universal “AI costs ₾X/month” figure.

Before setting public numbers, calculate each offer’s delivery time, revision allowance, subcontract/licence costs, warranty effort and contribution margin. Break-even is a floor, not a positioning strategy. A high-end studio homepage is itself a larger design investment; it does not establish that every client receives the same motion treatment.

### Lowering the barrier without discounting the whole business

Offer an initial message and a short fit conversation. The first paid project can be one tightly scoped website or workflow. For substantial unclear systems, a paid mapping phase may be appropriate if it produces a usable flow, priorities and build estimate; never insert it as a compulsory charge before hearing the request.

If a limited starter is useful, explain its scope and why it costs less. Avoid fake countdowns, perpetual discounts and a vague “unlimited automation” subscription. Subscription design examples are not a basis for promising unbounded operational support.

## K. Contact and conversion experience

NN/G recommends limiting contact forms to three to five fields and requesting information needed to respond [S38]. That supports a short form. It does **not** establish that a particular Genezisi headline or messaging channel will convert best.

### Recommended flow

Open a dedicated contact page or a clearly anchored form; use the same experience from every primary CTA. Keep a direct email route beside it. A verified business messenger may be added once the owner supplies the actual destination and wants to use it.

1. Optional topic buttons: **Website / AI assistant / Automation / Not sure**. No selection required.
2. Required message: **What would you like to improve or build?** Helper: “A sentence is enough. You don’t need to know the solution.”
3. Required reply address: email for the initial implementation. A channel chooser is useful only if those channels are actually supported and tested.
4. Optional name. Company/site URL and budget can be asked later when relevant.

Use visible labels and mark required fields explicitly. Do not rely on placeholder text. Keep the visitor’s message on validation or network errors. Show a success state only after the server acknowledges receipt; do not animate success while the request is still pending.

Success copy: “Your message has been received.” Add a response timeframe only after the owner establishes a reliable commitment. Offer a call as an optional next step, not a prerequisite to sending the request.

### Why the enquiry flow should remain human

The demo demonstrates AI; the contact flow should not require visitors to converse with a bot before reaching the founder. An assistant can optionally help prepare a message, but the form and direct route remain available.

### How to test conversion responsibly

Instrument CTA clicks, form starts, successful submissions and qualified enquiries by source and device. Count only acknowledged submissions as leads. Do not send raw enquiry text to general analytics.

Assess completed enquiries alongside relevance and follow-up outcomes. More messages with little project fit can increase workload without improving the business. With low traffic, run observed usability sessions and sequential changes; do not declare an A/B winner from a few conversions.

First comprehension check: after briefly viewing the hero, can a prospect say what Genezisi builds, identify one problem it could solve, and find a way to ask? Then observe the scroll sequence and contact task without explaining the design.

## L. Technical recommendation and acceptance criteria

The following is a proposed architecture. Confirm the existing repository, hosting and team workflow before migration; this is not an instruction to rewrite a working site merely to adopt a new framework.

### Default architecture

For a primarily editorial studio site, use **Astro with static content and a small interactive demo island**, plus **GSAP/ScrollTrigger for the signature scene**. Astro documents an islands architecture in which interactive components are hydrated independently [S32]. This supports the proposed separation between readable content and optional interactivity.

The demo can use a React island if its state management benefits from React. Plain TypeScript is sufficient for a small fixed sequence. Use DOM panels and SVG connectors first. All key copy remains HTML.

| Technology | Decision | Contribution / reason |
|---|---|---|
| Astro | Default for a new marketing-focused build | Pre-rendered editorial routes with isolated interactivity. |
| React | Optional within the demo | Manages alternate scenarios and state when complexity warrants it. |
| Next.js | Keep if the current site uses it effectively; choose for broader application requirements | Appropriate when the roadmap includes substantial authenticated/server application behavior. Verify current deployment constraints before selection. |
| GSAP + ScrollTrigger | Use for the signature sequence | Coordinated timelines, progress and bounded pinning [S31]. |
| CSS transforms / opacity | Use for controls and panel movement | Keep common feedback simple. Not every transform is automatically cheap; verify paint and compositing. |
| SVG | Preferred for request paths and diagram | Sharp, controllable and easy to pair with accessible DOM descriptions. |
| Lenis | Exclude initially | It is a smooth-scroll library [S36]; normal scrolling may already meet the design. Add only after a specific tested benefit. |
| Three.js / React Three Fiber | Conditional enhancement | Consider if a spatial prototype clearly surpasses the DOM version. 14islands documents progressive WebGL enhancement with its R3F scroll rig [S39]. Keep it behind a readable baseline. |
| WebGL shader | Optional and isolated | Potential surface/depth effect; never required to display the proposition or work. |
| Rive | Optional for reusable authored state animation | Its web runtime supports embedded Rive assets [S37]. A separate runtime is unnecessary for simple lines and panels. |
| Lottie | Omit in the initial direction | No identified need for a pre-authored vector clip; add only for an approved asset that offers a clear advantage. |
| View Transitions | Optional enhancement | API supports transitions between views [S35]. Use feature detection and immediate standard navigation as fallback. |
| CMS | Start with content files; introduce when editing needs justify it | Avoid operating another system merely to manage a few projects. |
| Contact backend | Required dependable service | Validate input, control abuse, acknowledge receipt and deliver enquiries. It operates independently of the animation. |

### Performance targets

Google’s good Core Web Vitals thresholds are LCP ≤2.5 seconds, INP ≤200ms and CLS ≤0.1, evaluated at the 75th percentile [S34]. These are targets to measure, not guarantees from a framework choice. Laboratory tests before launch and real-user data after launch have different roles.

Provisional project budgets, to be refined during the prototype:

- Core page JavaScript target around 100KB compressed; scene code split separately. Do not disguise deferred code as zero cost.
- Aim for initial visible assets under roughly 1MB on mobile; avoid hero video as a prerequisite.
- Font subsets and reserved image dimensions; check that Georgian subsets actually contain the required characters.
- No perpetual render loop while the scene is out of view or the tab is hidden.
- Approximately 60fps on representative devices where feasible; reduce visual fidelity before accepting long input stalls.
- If WebGL is adopted, cap pixel ratio, reduce effects on weaker devices and provide a static fallback on context loss. Device labels alone do not determine capability.

### Required verification

Test touch, keyboard, zoom, reduced motion, no-JS content, form failure, direct navigation, scrolling backward, resize and fast scrolling. Test real mobile Safari, Android Chrome and desktop Safari/Chrome/Firefox. Confirm form feedback does not vanish under the keyboard or sticky navigation.

Check crawlable titles/descriptions, locale routes, sitemap, canonical URLs, appropriate alternate-language references, meaningful heading order and project-page indexing. Do not make the demo the only explanation of any service.

For a live AI demo, additionally test unsupported requests, provider timeout, consumption limits and the handoff/static fallback. A demo that fails unpredictably is weaker evidence than an honest, well-made simulation.

## What exactly should we build?

**A dark, editorial, founder-led studio website with one compact enquiry-to-action reveal, real work immediately afterward, practical starting offers and a short message-first contact flow.**

The first screen sells considered website design. The first scroll reveals that Genezisi understands the business behind it. The first project proves actual delivery. The first contact requires only a request and a way to reply.

### Decision sequence before production code

1. Audit the actual Genezisi site, repository and available work. Confirm brand spelling, languages, audience, contact destinations and delivery capacity.
2. Replace hypothetical commercial assumptions with one initial customer profile and profitable offer scopes.
3. Assemble two or three honest case studies; select one sample scenario, labeled accurately.
4. Design desktop, mobile and reduced-motion storyboards. Start with a static hero and final system view; they must work before animation.
5. Build an isolated motion prototype for comparison and testing, not a production rewrite.
6. Test comprehension, contact usability and mobile performance. Shorten or simplify wherever the story obscures the service.
7. Turn the approved result into the V2 design specification, then implement and measure.

The concept earns its premium feeling through composition, pace and evidence. More animation is not an acceptance criterion. Clear understanding and a memorable demonstration are.
